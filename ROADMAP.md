# 🗺️ The Complete Software Engineering Roadmap — Days 31–90

**Days 1–30 taught you to earn from AI software. Days 31–90 make you undeniable.** Three dense phases that turn a 30-day builder into a complete, hire-ready software engineer — in three months total.

![The 90-day roadmap](images/roadmap.svg)

> **Why 3 months?** The field moves fast — a 6-month plan risks going stale before you finish it. This roadmap is compressed on purpose: lower-leverage topics cut, everything else taught at sprint pace. It assumes **3–5 hours a day** for days 31–90 (up from 60–90 minutes in the first 30 days). At one hour a day, this is still a 6-month plan — the roadmap is honest about that.

---

## What "feature-ready software engineer" actually means

Not a certificate. Not a tutorial count. A person who can do all of this:

- [ ] Build a full product end to end — frontend, API, database, deployed, with real users
- [ ] Explain every line they ship, in plain words
- [ ] Write tests that catch real breakage, and a pipeline that runs them on every push
- [ ] Design a database that won't collapse, with migrations
- [ ] Design a clean API strangers can use from docs alone
- [ ] Deploy with Docker + CI/CD, keep backups, survive the "server died" morning
- [ ] Keep secrets secret; name the likely attacks on their app and the defenses
- [ ] Read a big unfamiliar codebase and become useful in it within days
- [ ] Reason about cost growth and defend a system design out loud
- [ ] Work in a team: branches, PRs, review given and received
- [ ] Scope client work, write a contract, name a price, get a testimonial
- [ ] Present their work as case studies; survive interviews; negotiate an offer

If you can tick every box, you're not "learning to code" anymore. You're a software engineer.

## How the daily loop evolves

Days 1–30 ran on **60–90 minutes a day**: one concept, one build, one business lens. That loop still works — but the phases ask for more:

- **Phase 2:** 3–4 hours a day. Depth needs longer sessions.
- **Phase 3:** 4–5 hours a day. You're building a business-grade product in 20 days.
- **Phase 4:** the job hunt IS the work. Problems, mocks, applications, conversations — scheduled like a job.

The iron rule never changes: **never ship output you can't explain or defend.** It carried you through 30 days; it carries you through 90.

## What got cut (and why)

A 3-month plan can't hold a 6-month plan's content. Cut deliberately:

- **Deep language internals** → condensed to the 5 ideas you'll actually use, learned by applying.
- **100 interview problems** → 25, organized by pattern. Patterns beat volume.
- **Full DevOps mastery** → Docker + CI/CD + backups. Enough to look professional, not a career change.
- **The 2-week team simulation** → the essentials (tickets, PRs, review) practiced on real work.
- **Ten tutorials** → one. Building beats writing about building.

Nothing cut was load-bearing. Everything cut can be learned later, on the job, while getting paid.

## Honest expectations

- **This is a sprint, not a lifestyle.** 3–5 hours a day for 60 days is the price of 3 months. There's no version where it's 30 minutes a day.
- **The job hunt is part of the curriculum** (Phase 4, week 3), not something after it. Hiring takes weeks by itself — the funnel starts before day 90.
- **Freelancing and employment are parallel paths.** A retainer client in week 2 of Phase 4 is income AND proof.
- **You will feel behind at day 60.** Everyone does. The map is the antidote — follow it, tick the boxes, trust the process.
- **AI stays your junior developer the whole way.** The further you go, the more your value is judgment, not typing.
