# Mission 21: learn through the scene

Ask AI to produce useful checks

## Why this lesson

A useful test must be able to expose a real mistake. Checking a friendly message alone can miss an update to the wrong appointment.

## What to look for

Read the intended record and the actual changed record. Notice whether the selected check fails when the model has a defect.

## Start your project

Choose one important rule in your app. Ask AI for a check that passes when it works and fails when a deliberate mistake is introduced.

## Explain your own result

Explain what your check observes and show a defect that makes it fail. Keep unrun checks labelled as unrun.

## Your next steps

Use **Next: understand the idea**, explore the concepts, then **Build my project**. Copy the ready-to-use AI brief into your AI builder or coding assistant and ask how to open the result. Save your project observations, choose **Explain my system**, and then **Review my evidence**. Work through one question at a time. You can return later; a lesson can take more than one session.

## Try one change

Run the record assertion with the wrong-record defect present, then remove the defect.

First action: **Inspect the changed records**. The scene uses the same saved inputs and local rules as the original experiment. Changes appear immediately. Use either scene change, inspect a reading moment, or choose Follow the idea. Pause, restart and replay are available.

The illustration is a teaching view, not measured execution or real customer/provider evidence. The updater and assertions execute on two local fictional visits. This is a controlled defect, not a test of your real application.

## Your own project: Checks that can prove you wrong

Ask AI to create appropriate unit, integration and journey checks from the acceptance matrix. Demonstrate one real defect and keep its regression check.

Start here: Map important requirements to checks and expected observations.

Use Build this with AI to open the existing project. AI may implement everything. Explain the relevant input, rule and result in your own system; predict a changed case and check what actually happens. Keep your product observations separate from the teaching model.

Inputs and notes use the saved experiment and progress backup. Undo/reset remain available. Playback is temporary and never records mastery. Inspect the numbers and rules for full values. System/3D inspection and guided comparisons remain available.
