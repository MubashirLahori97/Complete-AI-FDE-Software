# Mission 28: Respond to reality

Stage 6: Deliver value and demonstrate ownership

An observed feedback cycle, a changed requirement and a bounded benefit claim.

AI can perform all implementation, research, planning, tests and diagnosis. Concepts are compulsory. This is a mission, not a one-day guarantee.

## The problem

The product works technically, but staff avoid it. Learn whether it improves the whole workflow, causes extra work elsewhere or misses a practical constraint.

## Understand the concepts

### Adoption, training and downstream effects

Adoption means people can and choose to use the result in their work. Training, trust, time and incentives affect it. A faster step can move a bottleneck or increase another person's workload. Observe the whole journey, not just the new screen.

**Example:** Dispatch saves time while technicians spend longer correcting incomplete access details.

**Common wrong idea:** A successful deployment proves useful adoption.

### Feedback, maintenance and causal limits

Feedback is evidence about experience and outcomes, with a source and context. Maintenance includes correcting defects and adapting to changed conditions. Compare outcomes carefully: selection, seasonality and other changes can explain a before/after difference.

**Example:** Report a small pilot's observed rate without claiming it proves the app caused every improvement.

**Common wrong idea:** Positive comments alone establish financial benefit.

## Predict, change, observe

The prepared pilot reduces dispatch time by 10 minutes per day. Change what happens downstream.

Prepared teaching simulation, not live provider or business evidence. Predict before selecting.

- **No extra downstream work:** A candidate improvement, subject to measurement and sample limits. Still check data quality and other changes before a causal claim.
- **20 extra minutes correcting visits:** The local speedup may worsen total workflow effort. Evaluate the whole outcome rather than the favoured step.
- **Staff do not use it:** Investigate access, fit, training and incentives before claiming impact. Technical availability and adoption differ.

Flow: Actual or labelled pilot use (People + workflow) → Feedback + outcome measures (Evidence record) → Adapt + recheck (Product + whole workflow)

## Understanding, separately from output

- **explain:** Explain the difference between deployment, adoption and demonstrated benefit.
- **locate:** Show evidence for a changed requirement and its resulting decision.
- **predict:** What if the faster workflow transfers work to another person?
- **compare:** Compare a training change with a software change for the observed confusion.
- **challenge:** AI says happier pilot users prove the product caused a 20% business improvement. What evidence is missing?

## Reviewer guidance

Offer contradictory pilot feedback or a different denominator and ask the learner to revise the conclusion.

AI stays available. A working output plus generated prose does not establish understanding if the learner cannot apply the concept. Practise these variations, then ask a reviewer for a fresh relevant variation:

- The most active users are also the most experienced staff. Reassess the comparison.
- The product meets its target but creates extra work for customers. Revise the guardrail.

Record who or what reviewed, the learner's predictions and reasoning, observed results, gaps and next practice. Local review records are not authenticated qualifications.
