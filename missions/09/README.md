# Mission 9: Compare proposed approaches

Stage 2: Understand the problem and design the product

A documented choice with costs, boundaries and a realistic alternative.

## The problem

The owner already pays for a scheduling product. A custom app is only one option. Compare buying, configuring, integrating and building against the actual workflow.

## Understand the concepts

### Buy, configure, integrate or build

Buying adopts a product, configuration adjusts supported behaviour, integration connects existing systems, and building creates new behaviour. Combinations are common. Compare task fit, access, export, maintenance and total cost rather than novelty.

**Example:** Configure an access-confirmation field before building a second scheduling system.

**Common wrong idea:** Custom software is automatically cheaper because AI writes the code.

### Boundaries and total cost

A boundary separates responsibilities. Every connection introduces a contract and a dependency. Total cost includes setup, usage, maintenance, migration and people's time. A low entry price can hide an expensive constraint.

**Example:** A free plan may lack the export or permissions the workflow needs.

**Common wrong idea:** A provider's current offer is a permanent guarantee.

## Predict, change, observe

Change the requirement before choosing an approach. These costs and offers are fictional.

Prepared teaching simulation, not live provider or business evidence. Predict before selecting.

- **Existing field fits:** Configure the current product and test the journey. The need may be met without a new system boundary.
- **Needs an external record:** Evaluate an integration and its API/export contract. Connecting systems may preserve useful existing workflows.
- **No suitable access/export:** Compare replacement and custom building with migration costs. A missing capability changes the feasible options, not just the screen design.

Flow: Workflow requirements (Product brief) → Compare options (Evidence + current provider terms) → Choose boundaries (Decision record)

**after:** Choose configuration for v1 and record what would trigger a replacement.

**lesson:** Check current capabilities and prices from the provider when doing the real project. The simulation is not market research.

## Project: A decision you can defend

Compare at least two plausible ways to deliver the brief, including an existing tool or configuration option. Choose a small reversible trial.

## Understanding, separately from output

- **explain:** Explain your approach and who owns each responsibility.
- **locate:** Find an integration boundary or the reason no integration is needed.
- **predict:** What happens if the provider removes a necessary export?
- **compare:** Defend the chosen approach against the strongest alternative, including maintenance.
- **challenge:** AI says free code generation makes ongoing cost zero. Name the missing costs and how to estimate them.

## Reviewer guidance

Introduce a changed budget or access constraint; check that the learner can revise the choice without defending a tool by habit.

AI stays available. A working output plus generated prose does not establish understanding if the learner cannot apply the concept. Practise these variations, then ask a reviewer for a fresh relevant variation:

- The current provider doubles its price. Recalculate the comparison.
- The team needs offline operation and a local language. Reassess suitability.

Record who or what reviewed, the learner's predictions and reasoning, observed results, gaps and next practice. Local review records are not authenticated qualifications.
