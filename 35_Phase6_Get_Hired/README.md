# 🎯 Phase 6 — Get Hired: Turn Proof Into a Paycheck *(Days 151–180)*

150 days of proof. **Now convert it into income.** Getting hired is a separate skill from building — this phase trains it like one. Four weeks, then the market decides.

---

## Week 1 — Portfolio that hires *(Days 151–157)*

**Goal:** make a stranger understand your value in 60 seconds.

**Learn:** the case-study format — problem → decisions → result · the 6-second resume scan (what hiring managers actually look at) · GitHub profile and LinkedIn cleanup · which 3 projects to lead with (and which to hide).

**Specify → AI builds:** AI drafts your case studies and resume; you rewrite every line until a non-technical friend "gets it" in 60 seconds. If they don't, it's not done.

**Evaluate:** the stranger test, again — show your profile to someone who doesn't know you. Can they say what you do and why it matters?

**💼 Business lens:** your portfolio is a sales page and you're the product. Most developers lose here before anyone reads their code.

**The one link:** [GitHub portfolio topic](https://github.com/topics/portfolio) — study real portfolios; steal the structure, not the content.

**Done when:**
- [ ] 3 projects rewritten as case studies (problem → decisions → result)
- [ ] One-page resume that survives the 6-second scan
- [ ] GitHub + LinkedIn cleaned up and consistent

---

## Week 2 — Interview prep system *(Days 158–164)*

**Goal:** treat interviews as a trainable skill, not a lottery.

**Learn:** coding rounds — thinking OUT LOUD while you code · take-homes — scoping ruthlessly to the time given · what interviewers actually score (it's not just correctness) · the questions YOU should ask them.

**Specify → AI builds:** AI plays interviewer — gives you problems, interrupts, asks "why?" Run 5 mock interviews. Then do one with a real human.

**Evaluate:** record one mock. Watch it. Count how many times you went silent. Silence is the thing to fix.

**💼 Business lens:** two equally skilled developers can have wildly different outcomes. The difference is almost always interview skill, and it's trainable.

**The one link:** [Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/) — the free, complete playbook.

**Done when:**
- [ ] 5 mock interviews done (4 with AI, 1 with a human)
- [ ] One recording watched, silence moments identified and worked on

---

## Week 3 — Stories and money talk *(Days 165–171)*

**Goal:** own your story and your price.

**Learn:** STAR stories from YOUR real projects (situation, task, action, result — with your Phase 2–5 work as the material) · salary research for your market · questions that reveal bad companies · negotiating without fear or apology.

**Specify → AI builds:** AI grills you on behavioral questions; you answer from real experience, not invented stories. You have 150 days of real material — use it.

**Evaluate:** write down your salary number, backed by data. Say it out loud until it stops feeling weird. That's the whole exercise.

**💼 Business lens:** the offer is the product of the entire 180 days. Developers who can't talk money leave 20–40% on the table at the only moment it matters.

**The one link:** [Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/) — real salary data, so your number is grounded, not guessed.

**Done when:**
- [ ] 6 STAR stories written from your real projects
- [ ] Your salary number written down, with data behind it, sayable out loud

---

## Week 4 — The funnel *(Days 172–180)*

**Goal:** run the job hunt like a system, not a hope.

**Learn:** 10 quality applications a week (quality beats quantity) · networking without cringe (help first, ask later) · referrals — why they work and how to earn them · tracking everything in one sheet · handling rejection without spiraling.

**Specify → AI builds:** AI tailors each application to the job post; you verify every claim is true. Never let AI invent experience — that's a career-ending lie.

**Evaluate:** the metric is INTERVIEWS HAPPENING, not applications sent. 20+ real applications, 5+ real conversations, pipeline reviewed weekly.

**💼 Business lens:** job hunting is sales. Sales is a numbers game played with a good product. You spent 150 days building the product; now sell it.

**The one link:** [Y Combinator jobs board](https://www.ycombinator.com/jobs) — real startup jobs, many open to strong juniors with proof.

**Done when:**
- [ ] 20+ quality applications sent, all tracked
- [ ] 5+ real conversations with humans at companies
- [ ] Interviews happening — the market is responding

---

## 🏁 Phase project — "The Offer"

Interviews in motion, or a signed freelance retainer. **This phase isn't done when you've applied — it's done when the market responds.** Keep the funnel running until it does. That's not failure; that's how hiring works.

---

## ⚠️ Traps to avoid

- **200 applications, weak portfolio.** Fix the product before scaling the sales. Ten great applications beat a hundred sprayed ones.
- **Waiting to feel "ready."** You won't. Ready is a story fear tells. The proof is already in your repos.
- **Ignoring freelancing.** A retainer client while you job-hunt is income AND proof. Parallel paths win.

## 🧠 Phase check

<details>
<summary><b>Q1.</b> Why do take-home assignments deserve ruthless scoping?</summary>

Because they're testing judgment, not endurance. A clean, well-scoped solution in 4 hours beats a sprawling mess in 20. Scope like a professional, not a student trying to impress.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Q2.</b> An interviewer asks "tell me about a time you failed." What are they actually testing?</summary>

Ownership and learning. They want: what broke, what was YOUR part in it, what you changed after. Your Phase retrospectives are full of real answers — use them.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Q3.</b> You have two offers: more money at a chaotic company, less at a healthy one. How do you think about it?</summary>

Money is one variable. Mentorship, code quality, and sanity compound for years; a 15% bump doesn't. Ask about on-call, code review, and why the last person left — then decide with data.
</details>
