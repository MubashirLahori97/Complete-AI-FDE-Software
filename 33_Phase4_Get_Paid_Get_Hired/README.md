# 🎯 Phase 4 — Get Paid & Get Hired: Turn Proof Into a Paycheck *(Days 71–90 · Weeks 11–13)*

Seventy days of proof. **Twenty days to convert it into income.** Condensed computer science for interviews, one real paid engagement, and the job hunt run as a system. The market decides at the end — your job is to give it no reason to say no.

**⏱ 3–5 hours a day.** Interviews are a separate skill. Train it like one.

## 🔭 This builds on

- **Day 22** — evals → now: proving your judgment in interviews, out loud
- **Day 26** — discovery questions → now: contracts, pricing, a paid engagement
- **Day 27** — packaging your work → now: case studies, STAR stories, the funnel

Part 1 taught you to earn. This phase teaches you to be undeniable.

## Week 1 — Think like a computer, condensed *(Days 71–77)*

**Goal:** the interview-proof core, without the 6-month grind.

**Learn:** Big-O in plain words — "how does the cost grow when the data grows?" · the core structures (arrays, strings, hash maps, trees, graphs — what they are, when they appear) · the 5 patterns behind most interview questions · recursion as "trust the smaller case" · the full URL journey (DNS → TCP → HTTP → TLS → pixels) · system design basics: caching, queues, load balancers, trade-offs out loud.

**Specify → AI builds:** 25 problems, pattern by pattern — YOU explain the approach first, AI checks your thinking. AI also plays system-design interviewer, pushing back ("what if this server dies?") while you defend your design.

**Evaluate:** for every solution, say WHY it's fast enough out loud. Two design docs: a URL shortener, and YOUR app at 1 million users — each defended for 10 minutes.

**💼 Business lens:** seniors are paid for judgment under uncertainty. This week is practice for the highest-paid skill in software.

**The one link:** [NeetCode](https://neetcode.io/) — problems organized by pattern, not by trick. (Deeper: [System Design Primer](https://github.com/donnemartin/system-design-primer), [the URL journey](https://github.com/alex/what-happens-when).)

**Done when:**
- [ ] 25 problems solved, each with a plain-words explanation of its cost
- [ ] 2 system design docs written and defended out loud

## Week 2 — Work like a professional *(Days 78–84)*

**Goal:** become someone companies and clients trust.

**Learn:** scoping work so it doesn't explode · pricing (hourly vs fixed vs value, in plain words) · what a contract must contain · saying no to bad work · team essentials: tickets, PRs, giving and receiving review without ego · teaching once — one public tutorial (teaching exposes every gap you still have).

**Specify → AI builds:** AI drafts your contract and proposal; you interrogate every clause until you understand what it protects. Never sign what you can't explain.

**Evaluate:** one paid engagement, end to end, WITH a written contract — and a testimonial at the end. Plus one public tutorial a stranger found useful.

**💼 Business lens:** this week can pay for the entire 90 days. Pricing confidence is a skill most developers never learn — and they leave money on the table forever.

**The one link:** [Freelancers Union](https://freelancersunion.org/) — free resources on contracts, rates, and not getting exploited.

**Done when:**
- [ ] One paid engagement completed with signed contract + testimonial
- [ ] One public tutorial published, with proof a stranger valued it

---

## Week 3 — Get hired *(Days 85–90)*

**Goal:** run the job hunt like a system, not a hope.

**Learn:** the case-study format (problem → decisions → result) · the 6-second resume scan · thinking OUT LOUD in coding rounds · STAR stories from YOUR real projects · your salary number, backed by data · the funnel: 10 quality applications/week, networking without cringe, referrals, tracking everything.

**Specify → AI builds:** AI tailors each application and plays interviewer for 3 mocks. You verify every claim is true — never let AI invent experience. That's a career-ending lie.

**Evaluate:** the metric is INTERVIEWS HAPPENING, not applications sent. 20+ real applications, 5+ real conversations, pipeline reviewed. Record one mock; fix the silences.

**💼 Business lens:** two equally skilled developers can have wildly different outcomes — the difference is almost always interview skill, and it's trainable. And the offer is the product of all 90 days: don't fumble the last step.

**The one link:** [Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/) — the free, complete playbook. (Salary data: [Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/).)

**Done when:**
- [ ] 3 projects rewritten as case studies + one-page resume + cleaned-up profiles
- [ ] 6 STAR stories written; salary number sayable out loud
- [ ] 20+ applications tracked, 5+ real conversations, interviews in motion

## 🏁 Phase project — "The Offer"

Interviews in motion, or a signed freelance retainer. **This phase isn't done when you've applied — it's done when the market responds.** Keep the funnel running until it does. That's not failure; that's how hiring works.

---

## ⚠️ Traps to avoid

- **200 applications, weak portfolio.** Fix the product before scaling the sales.
- **Waiting to feel "ready."** You won't. Ready is a story fear tells. The proof is in your repos.
- **Memorizing solutions.** If you can't re-derive it, you don't know it — interviews expose that in 30 seconds.
- **Undercharging.** "Exposure" doesn't pay rent. Your price rises with your proof.

## 🧠 Phase check

<details>
<summary><b>Q1.</b> Why only 25 problems instead of 100?</summary>

Patterns beat volume — returns collapse after the patterns click. Twenty-five well-understood problems teach more than a hundred memorized ones.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Q2.</b> An interviewer asks "tell me about a time you failed." What are they testing?</summary>

Ownership and learning: what broke, what was YOUR part, what you changed after. Your retrospectives are full of real answers — use them.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Q3.</b> You have 6 days left and no interviews yet. What's the move?</summary>

Don't spray 100 applications. Fix the weakest link: is the portfolio legible in 60 seconds? Are applications tailored? Ask one employed developer to review your resume. Then push the funnel harder.
</details>
